\section{Conclusions}
An S-band power amplifier was designed, fabricated and characterized. The experimental results at 2.2\,GHz are aligned with the output power and efficiency requirements, although the measured response is centered at 2.11\,GHz and 2.2\,GHz lies outside the $S_{11}<-10$\,dB bandwidth. Including the resistor ESL in the design model is expected to correct this shift, improving $S_{11}$ and $S_{21}$ at 2.2\,GHz in a future revision. A favorable compromise was obtained between output power and linearity, with a P1dB of 30.3 dBm and an OIP3 of 39.83 dBm. The drain efficiency reached 32.94\% at an output power of 30.9 dBm. The design parameters are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:pa_results}.

The measured output power meets the 1\,W requirement derived from the link budget, indicating that the amplifier is suitable for a 2.96\,Mbps QPSK downlink with a $10^{-5}$ BER at elevation angles above 20\degree. This communication performance follows from the link budget analysis, end-to-end validation with a modulated signal is left for future work.

Finally, the correlation between experimental results and simulated values confirms the proposed design methodology, consolidating a development and characterization platform for RF modules that can be reused in future aerospace applications.
